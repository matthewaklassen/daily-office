The Daily Office

Morning and Evening Prayer for the household, after the Book of Common Prayer of 1662, with the Scottish Psalter of 1650 and the Westminster Shorter Catechism

Editorial choices

  • Nothing in the offices is newly composed. Text in red italic is the prayer book's own rubric; notes marked ¶ are this edition's directions only.
  • The Absolution is appointed for a minister. In its place this office reads the Comfortable Words of Scripture, from the Communion, and prays the 1662 Collect for pardon and peace (the Twenty-first Sunday after Trinity).
  • The 1662 table of lessons includes readings from the Apocrypha. M'Cheyne's calendar, made for Scottish Presbyterian households, is used instead.
  • The collect of the day follows the 1662 Sundays and the chief feasts of Christ (Christmas, the Circumcision, the Epiphany, Ash Wednesday, Good Friday, Easter Even, Ascension Day). Saints' days are not observed.
  • The family and personal forms are shortened from the full office: the same texts, fewer of them, in the same order of call, confession, assurance, Word, prayer and blessing. They take the first psalm appointed for the day and one of the Comfortable Words in turn.
  • The psalms follow the 1662 order of thirty days, with the 1662 Proper Psalms on Christmas Day, Ash Wednesday, Good Friday, Easter Day, Ascension Day and Whitsunday. On the thirty-first the psalms of the thirtieth are read.
